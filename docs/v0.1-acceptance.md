# Scary Gary v0.1 acceptance contract

_Resolves [#11](https://github.com/scwlkr/scary-gary/issues/11). This contract defines release evidence; it does not replace the behavior, art, lifecycle, distribution, or implementation specifications that provide the exact commands and internals._

## Decision

Scary Gary v0.1 is **Rough-complete** only when every promised behavior is recognizable on its Primary Scene, exits coherently, cleans up under every interruption, and uses its documented Safe Fallback when an environmental prerequisite is unavailable. A missing behavior, unsafe side effect, input/focus interference, broken cleanup, or fallback used in place of a valid Primary Scene blocks release.

Four verdicts keep “rough” from becoming a feature waiver:

- **Required**: failure blocks v0.1.
- **Required fallback**: loss of an environmental prerequisite may reduce visual fidelity, but the specified harmless outcome must pass. The feature's Primary Scene must also pass in an environment where its prerequisites exist.
- **Polish debt**: a visible imperfection may ship only when identity, timing contract, safety, input pass-through, cleanup, and feature recognizability remain intact.
- **Unsupported**: no feature behavior is promised, but Gary's installer or process must refuse, exit, or remain hidden without persistence, damage, focus theft, input capture, or a retry loop.

The tested release artifact is the published `win-x64` `gary.exe`, not a debugger build. All hands-on results must identify the executable SHA-256, source commit, Windows edition/build, display topology and scale, and evidence link. A result from a different binary does not transfer.

## Feature acceptance matrix

| ID | Promise | Required Primary Scene | Required exit, interruption, and fallback | Allowed polish debt |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F00 | Startup and safe loiter | After the specified hidden 30–90-second Starting delay, Gary appears at a safe taskbar or monitor-edge anchor and remains available for scheduling. | Stop, Esc, lock, secure desktop, no usable monitor, or exclusive full screen removes every Gary-owned visual and sound. Geometry loss causes clean disappearance and later neutral re-entry after two stable seconds. | Minor anchor imprecision or a visibly simple idle loop. |
| F01 | Taskbar walk | Gary walks 20–60% of a stable taskbar or monitor edge for 8–18 seconds, then loiters. | Requery changed geometry; use the monitor edge when taskbar geometry is absent or ambiguous. Never walk off a usable monitor or block taskbar input. | Feet may not meet every third-party taskbar theme perfectly. |
| F02 | Start-button sit | On a supported ordinary taskbar, Gary moves to the best documented Start-area approximation, sits for 8–15 seconds, then gets up. | Use the nearest taskbar corner when the Start position cannot be inferred; make the action ineligible when no stable edge exists. Never inspect Explorer child windows. | Several logical pixels of Start-anchor error. Exact Start-button occlusion is not promised. |
| F03 | Gary Doze | Gary visibly enters, holds, and wakes from a 15–30-second doze at a safe anchor. | Cancellation removes the doze immediately. Gary Doze never hides Gary for three hours or changes Gary Mode to Garynap. | Rough pose transitions or one simple hold loop. |
| F04 | Edge peek | Gary partially emerges from a usable edge, watches for 3–7 seconds, and retreats. | Geometry invalidation completes the retreat when possible, otherwise hides Gary immediately. | Coarse clipping or limited edge-specific frame variety. |
| F05 | Behind-window crawl | With a suitable visible uncloaked ordinary window, Gary approaches an edge, clips away, waits 1–3 seconds, reappears beyond the opposite edge, and continues. | A moved, closed, minimized, cloaked, unsafe, or always-on-top target makes Gary finish disappearing and not reappear. Gary never reparents into or changes the target window or shared z-order. | The clip need not trace a complex non-rectangular window outline. |
| F06 | Sprint | Gary crosses one monitor in 1–3 seconds and exits beyond its edge. | A topology or safety change ends the sprint cleanly; Gary never crosses monitor boundaries mid-action. | Rough acceleration or a small route-height mismatch. |
| F07 | Fall | Gary runs or shuffles, trips, lies still for 2–5 seconds, rises, and loiters. | Any immediate cancellation removes the fall pose and its audio. | Limited in-between frames. |
| F08 | Cursor stare | A cursor lingering within 300 logical pixels for 1.5 seconds causes a 2–5-second head/eye stare. | Leaving range, losing the input desktop, changing monitor, or interruption ends the reaction without hooks, input capture, or visible retries. | Coarse look-direction buckets. |
| F09 | Cursor follow | After the nearby cursor moves away at an ordinary pace, Gary follows visibly for 3–7 seconds. | Stop at a monitor boundary or unsafe geometry; never teleport between monitors. | Coarse distance keeping or reuse of the walk loop. |
| F10 | Cursor flee | A rapid approach within 120 logical pixels makes Gary run to the nearest safe edge and normally disappear within two seconds. | An unavailable route causes immediate clean disappearance. The cursor remains fully usable. | Simple route selection or reused sprint frames. |
| F11 | Garbage drag | Gary carries one bundled trash prop along a clear lower-screen route for 8–15 seconds and takes it away. | The prop disappears with Gary on every exit and never becomes a file or desktop item. | One trash design, rough hand alignment, or placeholder-quality bundled rustle. |
| F12 | Fake icon theft | In a stable ordinary desktop environment, Gary stages and carries away the bundled generic mystery-file prop in 8–12 seconds. | Abandon the action if it would overlap real content or the environment becomes invalid. Never enumerate, name, image, hide, move, or imitate a real icon. | The prop need not align to the user's icon grid. |
| F13 | Signs | Each of the twelve approved messages can be selected, raised for 4–8 seconds, and lowered with exact text and tone. | Every sign disappears on interruption; no message uses private data or personalization. | One board design, rough arm alignment, or basic typography that remains legible. |
| F14 | Gary Event | `gary.exe --rehearse-event` proves the production sequence on the cursor monitor: one 300 ms black blink, exactly three silent Gary-absent seconds, then the canonical face and one bundled sound together for 900 ms, followed by immediate cleanup and neutral return. Normal scheduling separately proves the eight-awake-hour gate, 1/3600 eligible-minute hazard, consume-at-blink persistence, and 72-hour cooldown. | Stop, Esc, unsafe environment, or invalid topology immediately removes all Event visuals/audio; once the blink begins the cooldown remains consumed. Missing/muted audio never delays, repeats, or cancels the visual. Other topmost or secure surfaces may remain above Gary. | Up to four logical pixels of face jitter and ordinary scheduler/timer measurement tolerance outside the exact authored durations. The sequence, face, sound asset, monitor scope, and cleanup are not polish. |

For F01–F14, the accepted art identity, frame/prop inventory, manifest pivots, mirror permissions, sign faces, alpha/export checks, and sound rules from [#7](https://github.com/scwlkr/scary-gary/issues/7) are part of the required feature, not an optional art pass. Routine Foley may be placeholder-quality, but it must remain bundled, local, quiet, Gary-only, and subject to the specified 25% chance.

| E14 | Esc during Starting, Awake, and Gary Event; Esc again during Garynap | The first Esc is observed without consuming it, immediately removes Gary-owned output, and begins one three-hour Garynap. | E15 | End process in Task Manager | Gary's process, windows, props, and audio end immediately. No service, helper, scheduled task, or watchdog restores him in the same session; registered Startup may launch him at the next sign-in. | Required |
| E16 | Disable Gary Startup, then sign-out/sign-in | The current Gary Session continues, but no Gary process starts at the next sign-in. Disabling persistence never requires administrator access and is not polled, repaired, or overwritten behind the user's back. | Required |
| E17 | Run Gary Removal twice | Gary Removal disables Startup first, requests a graceful session stop without force-killing, and clears Gary-owned per-user state after exit; the second run is an idempotent no-op. If stop or cleanup fails, persistence stays disabled, the command reports failure, and a safe retry remains possible. | Required |
| E18 | Executable missing while a stale Startup entry exists | Nothing respawns, repairs, downloads, or retries. The documented manual per-user cleanup removes the stale registration without the executable or elevation. | Required safe failure |
| E19 | Recoverable fault, repeated presentation failure, and forced process crash | A recoverable action/adapter fault invalidates only the affected action and returns through a Safe Fallback with bounded retry. The lifecycle itself is fixed by [#8](https://github.com/scwlkr/scary-gary/issues/8). Public command names and disclosure belong to [#9](https://github.com/scwlkr/scary-gary/issues/9); control-channel and state-storage mechanics belong to [#10](https://github.com/scwlkr/scary-gary/issues/10). Those choices must satisfy E13–E20 without weakening these observable outcomes.

## Focused automated evidence

The implementation roadmap must bind each gate below to a repeatable command. Before v0.1 completion, the evidence record must show all tests passing against the release source commit:

| Gate | Minimum automated proof |
| --- | --- |
| A01 — Catalog completeness | A contract test enumerates F01–F14 and proves every action has tone, cooldown, prerequisites, entrance, performance, exit, interruption cleanup, Safe Fallback, required atlas clips/props, and audio rule. It also proves all twelve exact sign messages exist. |
| A02 — Mode and priority model | Fake-clock and seeded-random tests cover every valid/invalid Gary Mode transition, fixed signal priority, 30–90-second Starting delay, 10–45-second quiet gap, two-second stability gate, recency relaxation, cooldown classes, and 70/20/9 eligible action weighting without treating it as a quota. |
| A03 — Action timing and invalidation | Parameterized tests cover every duration/threshold in F01–F13, safe exit within two seconds for queued reactions/Event, immediate cleanup for Stop/Esc/unsafe environment, and removal of every Gary Prop/audio handle. |
| A04 — Gary Event | Fake-clock/RNG and restart tests prove the eight-awake-hour accumulator, 1/3600 per-eligible-minute hazard, persist-at-blink behavior, 72-hour wall-clock cooldown, exact 300 ms + 3 s + 900 ms sequence, interruption, missing audio, and the history-free `--rehearse-event` path. |
| A05 — Geometry and DPI | Pure geometry tests cover signed negative coordinates, 100/150/200% conversion, monitor choice, work-area/taskbar inference, safe anchors, per-monitor clamping, topology invalidation, and cursor-monitor Event bounds. |
| A06 — Shell, target, and z-order adapters | Windows integration tests use controlled windows/shell facts to reject Gary, hidden, minimized, zero-area, cloaked, off-desktop, and unsafe topmost targets; refresh on shell/display/settings changes; apply click-through, no-activate, tool-window, and bounded topmost styles; and bound retries until an environment change or backoff expires. |
| A07 — Lifecycle and failure | Disposable per-user tests cover single-instance launch without state reset, exact Startup add/remove/disable authority, idempotent non-self-deleting Gary Removal, missing executable/inert registration, in-place and path-changing upgrade, conservative missing/corrupt/incompatible state, persistent-write Event disablement, repeated presentation failure, process crash, no restart/helper, and preservation of Event cooldown where required. |
| A08 — Asset and package contract | Build validation checks every approved clip/frame budget, pivot/socket, mirror permission, exact sign face, PNG alpha/matte/padding rule, WAV format/level metadata, embedded standalone Event assets, and a one-file self-contained `win-x64` publish. |
| A09 — Safety boundary | Tests and a dependency/API audit prove Gary does not read or mutate Desktop items, other files/content, display settings, system volume, foreign window state, global hooks, admin resources, or the network. |

Timing tests use a fake monotonic clock; stochastic tests use an injected seeded source. Wall-clock sleeps and probabilistic “it happened once” tests do not count as deterministic evidence.

Automated gates do not prove desktop composition, physical scale, focus, input pass-through, audible balance, or the joke's recognizability. Record one checklist/video or screenshot sequence per run below, using disposable VMs or physical machines. VM evidence is sufficient when the display/topology limitation is stated.

| Run | Required setup and observations |
| --- | --- |

| H02 — Mixed-display movement | Windows 11 with two displays at 100%/150%, one using negative virtual coordinates. Exercise walk, peek, sprint, all cursor reactions, crawl, display add/remove/reorder, cursor-monitor selection, and monitor-local Event bounds. |

Repeat launch, representative Primary Scenes from each family (movement, staged action, prop, cursor, sign), taskbar placements exposed by that OS, mixed-DPI or 150% geometry, Event rehearsal, Startup, Task Manager stop, and removal. | H05 — Lifecycle and failure | On both promised Windows generations, verify Startup sign-in and duplicate launch, Esc cleanup in Starting/Awake/Event, Garynap non-extension, Task Manager termination, next-sign-in return, Startup disablement without stopping the current session, recoverable target/shell failure, forced crash cleanup without restart, stale registration cleanup, upgrade, and two Gary Removal runs that leave the portable executable. ## Completion rule

v0.1 may be called complete only when:

1. A01–A09 pass on the release commit and H01–H06 pass on the same published executable checksum.
2. Every F00–F14 and E01–E21 row has a linked result; no required row is failed, skipped, or replaced by a fallback-only demonstration.
3. Any remaining debt is itemized and fits only the “Allowed polish debt” column. Missing art, behavior, timing, safety, cleanup, lifecycle, support, or removal is not debt.
4. Failures are fixed and the affected focused gate plus the complete automated suite are rerun. A waiver, flaky retry, or unsupported label cannot convert a promised case into a pass.
5. The public support statement names the tested Windows builds and the .NET 10 Windows 10 caveat, the checksums match the reviewed artifact, and the owner accepts the evidence before the v0.1 tag is created.

This contract deliberately makes visual fidelity best-effort only where Windows exposes no stable primitive. Harmlessness, complete feature presence, deterministic lifecycle, and clean restoration are always release requirements.
